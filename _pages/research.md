My central interest is **reliable AI for visual and spatial data**. Professional work in computer vision, GIS and photogrammetry has shown me how acquisition and processing decisions affect downstream interpretation. I want to develop and evaluate methods that turn imagery and 3D observations into dependable map information.

## Geospatial AI and GIS

I have worked on GIS digitization, road-asset imagery and multi-year orthophoto analysis for building construction, demolition and extension. These tasks combine visual evidence with spatial context and often need careful human review.

**Research question:** How can a change-detection method distinguish genuine urban change from differences caused by registration, resolution, viewing conditions or acquisition year?

A first study could compare a standard bitemporal baseline with a quality-aware method, then evaluate on spatially separated areas. Useful outputs would include class-specific errors, uncertainty and map-ready features rather than a single overall score. This is a proposed research direction, not a completed benchmark.

## Photogrammetry and 3D mapping

My photogrammetry work has included AT-related workflows, DSM/DTM preparation, image rectification, seamline editing, tiling, bridge editing and final orthophoto output. I later worked on point-cloud alignment across multiple trajectories, segmentation and classification. I also have experience in algorithm development and system design for computer vision and GIS applications.

**Research question:** How do geometric alignment and reconstruction errors affect object extraction and map updates, and can semantic consistency help expose those errors?

I am interested in methods that evaluate geometry and interpretation together, using independent geometric checks when suitable reference data exist. The goal is to understand when a final mapping product can be trusted and when it needs review.

## Biomedical signal processing

My IUT undergraduate thesis investigated EEG analysis in the context of impaired consciousness and coma. I also co-authored a conference paper on explainable retinal OCT classification. These experiences motivate a separate interest in interpretable biomedical signal and image analysis. A new biomedical study would need modality-specific data, appropriate validation and, for clinical questions, relevant collaboration.

## Computer vision and vision-language systems

My applied computer-vision work includes road imagery, building-change interpretation, point-cloud classification and a prior traffic-detection project. I am interested in systems that can describe visual evidence and uncertainty for a reviewer. Vision-language and LLM research is a **proposed extension** of this background; I do not present it as a completed LLM project.
